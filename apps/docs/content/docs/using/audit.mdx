title: The audit trail

Most systems drop what they blocked. That is exactly the record you want three
weeks later, when someone asks what a line on an invoice was for.

Pocket keeps all of it.

## Payments

Open **Payments** for every attempt, including the ones that never went through.
Filter by agent or by status.

<Shot
  src="payments"
  alt="The payments screen listing attempts with time, agent, amount, category, recipient and status, refusals tinted red."
  caption="Payments — refusals stay in the list rather than being dropped from it"
/>

| Status            | Means                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Blocked           | Refused by a limit you set            |
| Awaiting approval | Held for you. Nothing signed          |
| Approved          | Allowed, and on its way to the seller |
| Submitted         | Sent, waiting on confirmation         |
| Settled           | Paid and delivered                    |
| Failed            | Allowed, but did not go through       |

Open any row for the full detail: the seller, the reason your agent gave, the
rule that stopped it if it was stopped, and a link to the transaction.

Held purchases are approved or rejected from here.

## Why refusals are kept

A refusal is a fact about your system, not an error that failed to happen.

<Shot
  src="payment-blocked"
  alt="An expanded blocked payment showing its id, initiator, reason, resource, recipient, network, category, task budget and refusal code."
  caption="One refusal, expanded — the reason is the line worth reading"
/>

```text
Refused      Full market report
Agent        Hermes
Amount       0.75 USDC
Seller       market-data.example.com
Rule         per-job budget
Left         0.03 of 0.50
Asked by     agent, unattended
When         Tue 09:14:22
```

The "left" line matters as much as the rule. An agent told what it had can pick
something cheaper. A person reading it later can tell whether the limit was too
tight or the agent was being unreasonable.

## The full record

**Audit** shows everything, newest first, not just payments. Budget changes,
policy changes, new agents, new keys, approvals and rejections. Narrow it by
activity — payments, agents, budgets, task budgets, policies, API keys, wallets,
the organization itself — or by who acted, and page back through as far as the
record goes.

<Shot
  src="audit"
  alt="The audit screen listing events by time, actor, action and details, with filters for activity and actor."
  caption="Audit — every actor and every action, newest first"
/>

Each entry names who did it:

| Who    | Means                                 |
| ------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Agent  | An agent acting on its own            |
| Human  | Someone signed in to the dashboard    |
| System | Pocket itself, recording a settlement |

A purchase you make from the dashboard and one your agent makes go through
identical checks. Who asked is the only difference recorded, which is why it is
recorded rather than guessed at.

## Spend summaries

The **Overview** screen rolls it up: spend over the last seven days, today's
figures against your ceiling, spend by category, and anything unusual.

<Shot
  src="overview-payments"
  alt="Spend by category as a stacked bar with a ranked list, above a table of the ten most recent payments."
  caption="Overview — what the money went on, and the last ten attempts"
/>

Today's numbers sit apart from the period's on purpose. An agent can be well
inside its weekly average and still have spent its whole day by lunchtime.

Your agent can read the same summary through `pocket_spend_summary`, so you can
ask it directly:

```text
Summarise what agent agent_1a2b3c spent in the last 7 days, broken down
by category. List anything that was blocked and why.
```

## What is never recorded

No key, no token, and no credential of any kind appears in the audit trail or in
any log. There is nothing there to leak, because Pocket never holds signing
material in the first place.
